# VAERIQ Operator Validation Outreach — M04

## Objective

Find treasury/finance/payment operators with direct experience managing stablecoin or on-chain payment workflows.

The goal of the first contact is problem discovery, not product adoption.

## Recommended funnel

Public Builder Update / X → 15–20 minute research conversation → workflow + concrete incident → VAERIQ fit discussion → optional prototype/demo → concrete workflow test only when justified.

Do not reverse this order by asking cold contacts to try VAERIQ before understanding their workflow.

## Channel 1 — Colosseum Builder Update

Suggested copy:

> VAERIQ is entering the operator-validation phase.
>
> The product is built around a simple control question: before an on-chain payment moves, does the system know the intent, business context, evidence, policy constraints, and risk?
>
> I'm looking to speak with treasury / finance / payment operators who work with stablecoins or on-chain payments.
>
> 15–20 min. No sales pitch. I want to understand how payments are actually requested, approved, executed, and reconciled today — including what existing controls already solve well.
>
> If this is your workflow, reply here or DM me. A short form is also available for scheduling.

Keep the post focused on who we want to learn from and what the conversation is for. Do not claim customer demand or validation from the outreach itself.

## Channel 2 — X

>
> Looking for 15–20 min conversations about real approval, payment, and reconciliation workflows. No sales pitch.
>

The VAERIQ account can emphasize the product question; the founder account can make the request personal.

## Channel 3 — Direct outreach

Prioritize warm introductions and people whose public profile clearly shows treasury, finance, payments, stablecoin operations, or protocol finance responsibilities.

A public Colosseum Arena profile example exposes role/interests and a Message action, so relevant Arena profiles can be a practical direct-outreach surface where available. Verify each person's actual role before contacting them.

Suggested DM:

> Hey — I'm building VAERIQ, a control layer for on-chain/stablecoin payments.
>
> I'm doing a small round of operator interviews to understand how treasury/payment approvals actually work today.
>
> I'm not looking to pitch the product. I'd mainly like to learn about your workflow, existing controls, and any real payment failures or reconciliation pain you've seen.
>
> Would you be open to a 15–20 min conversation?

## Short intake form

Keep the form to 5–7 questions.

1. What best describes your role? (Treasury / Finance / Payments / Operations / Protocol-foundation finance / Other)
2. What kind of payment workflow do you operate? (Vendor payments / Payroll or contractor payments / Treasury transfers / Stablecoin settlement / Other)
3. Which best describes your experience? (Regularly operate / Oversee / Build software used by these workflows / Other)
4. What would you like to discuss? (Approval controls / Payment risk / Stablecoin operations / Reconciliation / Automated or agentic payments / Other)
5. Preferred contact handle/email.
6. Optional: How long is your typical payment workflow?
7. Optional: One sentence on the hardest part of controlling or reconciling these payments today.

Do not collect wallet addresses, credentials, private financial records, invoices, transaction secrets, or other sensitive customer information.

## Qualification rule

A form response is a lead for research, not validation.

Prioritize conversations where the person directly operates or oversees relevant workflows, can describe a real current process, can discuss a concrete recent failure or friction, and can explain existing controls.

Do not prioritize based only on title or enthusiasm.

## After the conversation

Record the interview in docs/CUSTOMER_VALIDATION_LOG.md.

Classify each finding as Observed / Reported / Inferred.

Then map only supported findings into Intent → Context → Evidence → Policy → Risk → Decision → Reconciliation.

If the operator workflow does not map cleanly, record the mismatch rather than forcing it into the model.

## Follow-up ladder

Conversation → Follow-up/demo → Technical workflow review → Concrete test with non-production funds → Defined pilot/integration experiment.

A person saying “this sounds useful” is not a design-partner commitment.

## M04 target

- 5–10 relevant operator conversations
- recurring workflow/failure patterns
- 2–3 serious design-partner candidates where evidence supports them
- at least one concrete workflow commitment where possible

These are investigation targets, not guaranteed outcomes.
